# Topic 6: GEOMETRY and TOPOLOGY (Phase 5A & 5B)

The flowchart specifically outlines the structural bridge from raw UNet pixels into clean, CAD-grade architecture. This is handled by two distinct but highly dependent phases: **GEOMETRY** (Pixel to Line Conversion) and **TOPOLOGY** (Line to Structure Logic).

---

## 1. GEOMETRY Phase: Pixel-Cloud PCA Line Fitting

In traditional vision pipelines, developers take the 1-pixel wide skeleton and run the **Hough Transform** to find lines. However, Hough Transforms often hallucinate lines or miss subtle connections. 
Our architecture abandons Hough completely for a much more robust "Skeleton-to-Cloud" approach.

### Step 1: Skeleton Topology Extraction
We thin the raw Unet mask down to a 1-pixel-wide line (Skeletonization). We then use the **Ramer-Douglas-Peucker (RDP)** algorithm to chop wild, curved pixel lines into completely straight "Sub-Segments".
*Note: This gave us straight lines, but they are based on a 1-pixel wide skeleton, which is extremely noisy and can wiggle back and forth improperly inside thick walls.*

### Step 2: Pixel-Cloud Density Extraction
Instead of trusting the wiggly skeleton line, we use the skeleton sub-segment purely as a "guide rail".
We traverse the guide segment and mathematically sweep outward by a small $radius$ (e.g., $7\text{px}$) into the *original raw UNet mask*. We physically map out the exact location of every single "Wall Pixel" that physically exists within that spatial corridor. We refer to this raw array of pixels as the **Pixel Cloud**.

### Step 3: PCA (Principal Component Analysis) Line Fitting
Now that we have a dense cluster of physical wall pixels, we want to draw the absolute theoretically perfect straight line straight through the center of its mass.
- **Mathematical Principle:** We use Euclidean Distance Line Fitting ($L2$-norm minimization), equivalent intuitively to Principal Component Analysis (finding the vector direction that maximizes the variance of the data distribution).
- **Code Function:** `fit_line_to_cloud()` in `pipeline.py` using `cv2.fitLine(pixel_cloud, cv2.DIST_L2, 0, 0.01, 0.01)`.
- **Result:** OpenCV evaluates thousands of raw pixels in the cloud, minimizes their perpendicular distances to a hypothetical center, and outputs a mathematically flawless unit vector `(vx, vy)` and a point `(x0, y0)` on the line. We project the start/end points of the original subset onto this mathematical line. 

*Conclusion:* We have turned messy pixels into theoretically perfect, pristine CAD vectors.

## 2. TOPOLOGY Phase: Snapping and Welding

The vectors generated by the Geometry phase are incredibly accurate, but they do not know about each other. Lines meant to intersect at a corner might physically miss each other by 2 pixels. Architectural spaces must be mathematically sealed ("watertight") or BIM extrusion fails.

### Step 1: KD-Tree based Local Corner Welding (Centroid Snapping)
- **The Issue:** A floorplan with hundreds of vectors has $2N$ endpoints. Checking every endpoint against every other endpoint computationally costs $O(N^2)$, which is devastating for massive graphs.
- **The KD-Tree Optimization:** We load every point into a $k$-dimensional spatial tree data structure (`KDTree`). This allows us to instantly query any points within a specific `SNAP_THRESHOLD` locally in $O(N \log N)$ time.
- **Union-Find Constraints:** Two endpoints within $10\text{px}$ of each other belong to the same corner. We use a **Disjoint-Set (Union-Find)** algorithm to physically "weld" them. We calculate the absolute centroid (average $X$ and $Y$) of the cluster and overwrite every coordinate in the cluster to this exact new `(X, Y)`. The geometric gap snaps shut structurally.

### Step 2: Local Orthogonal Snapping (Manhattan enforcement)
- **The Calculation:** Even with perfect line fitting, a horizontal wall might mathematically slope by $1^\circ$ across 40 feet. We run an algorithm to dynamically check every vector's slope: `np.arctan2(dy, dx)`.
- **The Enforcement:** If the absolute difference between the angle and any cardinal direction ($0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$) is less than $5^\circ$ (`ANGLE_TOLERANCE`), we mathematically delete its slope. For horizontal lines, we calculate the `mean_y` and force exactly $y_1 = \text{mean\_y}$ and $y_2 = \text{mean\_y}$. The wall is now theoretically infinite structurally.

### Step 3: Orthogonal Line Intersections (Gap Closing)
Sometimes, walls are further apart than the `SNAP_THRESHOLD`. Specifically, at T-Junctions where an internal wall terminates $15\text{px}$ before touching an exterior load-bearing wall.
- **Linear Algebra Extension:** For every endpoint, we check if it is floating. We find the nearest perpendicular wall (verified via taking the dot-product of their matrices $\approx 0$).
- **$Y = mx + b$ intersection:** We mathematically define an infinite line equation for the floating vector, and an infinite line equation for the target wall. We calculate their theoretical 2D intersection coordinate and extend the floating vector exclusively to that point. The T-Junction geometrically seals mathematically.
